# Educator review packet — Chef Capy counting 1–5

**For a kindergarten educator. About 20 minutes to read; the questions are
in section 7 and the record is in section 8.**

This is a development prototype on one device, with fictional children. It
has not been reviewed by an educator or tried with children, and nothing in
it is approved. Please judge it as it is. The fields at the end stay blank
until a reviewer fills them in.

What it covers, as implemented in version 0.9.1:

- the activity: `slice-serve.count-1-5`, version 1.0.0;
- the proposed rule: r1;
- the lesson template: t1.

To try it: open <https://morecobrax-dot.github.io/Capybara-Sushi/> on a
tablet held sideways and tap an avatar. The teacher's side is behind the
grown-ups' button (top corner), then the times-table question, then
**Teacher workspace**. The class menu there has **Willow class (DEMO sample
data)**, which is the fictional class used in the examples below.

## 1. The objective, and what the child sees and does

**Objective:** "Make and serve a set of one to five sushi pieces that matches
the quantity a customer asks for."

**What the child sees.**
- A cat customer at the side of the screen, with a speech bubble.
- In the bubble, one to five maki drawn as pictures: in a row, in a dice
  pattern, or scattered. No numeral is ever shown.
- If the device has a voice of its own and sound is on, Chef Capy also says
  the order ("Three pieces, please"). Many devices have none; the picture
  then carries the order alone.

**What the child does.**
1. Cuts slices off a sushi roll, by swiping down across it or pressing the
   knife button.
2. Moves one slice at a time onto the plate, by tapping it or pressing the
   plate button. The child can take a piece back the same way.
3. Rings the bell when ready.

Only a slice can go on the plate. The uncut rest of the roll stays where it
is until it is sliced. Nothing is served until the bell, and there is no
timer.

**What counts as correct.** The number of pieces on the plate at the bell
equals the number asked for. Piece sizes, how many cuts were made, speed and
neat slicing never matter.

**A round** is five orders: each quantity from 1 to 5 once, in a shuffled
order. At the end the child chooses more orders or finish.

**What the game can and cannot show.** "The game records the set a child
serves: whether the pieces on the plate match the picture order, with or
without help. It does not show how the set was made — counting aloud,
touching each piece once, or seeing the amount at a glance — or whether the
child reads numerals; none are shown. How a child counts is for the
teacher's own observation, kept apart."

## 2. Demonstration and help

**Demonstration.** Once, when the child goes in, Chef Capy shows the task
with a pointing hand: two slices cut off the roll, one tapped onto the
plate, the other sent with the plate button, then the bell. It can be
skipped. It is never evidence.

**Help.** A child's first different answer is never shown as wrong; help
comes, then another try.

| Step | When | What happens | Reveals the answer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repeat the order | Any time (tap the order card) | The order is shown and said again | No: ordinary instruction |
| Count together | After the first different serve, or when the child asks for help | Chef Capy counts the order's pictures, then the pieces on the plate, one at a time | Yes |
| Rings on the plate | After a second different serve | The plate shows a ring for each piece still wanted | Yes |
| Chef Capy makes it | After a third different serve | Chef Capy makes the order and serves it; the order ends | Yes |

## 3. What counts as what

| Kind | Meaning | Counted as |
|---|---|---|
| Independent first answer | The first set served for an order, with no help before it | Evidence for the rule |
| Supported answer | Any later serve, after help | Shown separately as supported retries; never added to the independent count |
| Assisted-only order | Help asked for before the first serve | Not independent; shown beside the status |
| Interrupted or unfinished | The child left before serving (for example, the grown-ups' button was opened) | Excluded; never counted as incorrect |
| Excluded | The demonstration, a teacher's preview, repeats of the order, the same event received twice, an identical order replayed in one round, days outside the class calendar | Not evidence |
| Teacher observation | What the teacher writes down from watching | A separate record; it never changes the game's counts |

## 4. The proposed rule r1 (not validated)

"At least 12 usable independent first responses, across at least two
sessions on different instructional dates, covering at least four
quantities including 5, with at least 80% correct."

It is read over the class calendar's last 14 instructional days.

| Status | When | Suggested next step |
|---|---|---|
| Not observed | No usable counting orders in the window | A short observation: watch the child make a set from a mixed pile |
| Gathering evidence | Some orders, but not yet enough for the rule | More counting orders, on another instructional day |
| Building | The rule has enough evidence, but under 80% matched | Model touching each piece once, then practice sets of 3 to 5 |
| Independent in game | The rule is met, with 80% or more | A transfer check with real objects, before the next skill |
| Needs a fresh check | Met only before this window | A brief counting check before moving on |

"With support" is never assigned until a support rubric for this skill has
been reviewed.

## 6. One lesson, with a physical transfer check (template t1)

The app's draft for the 20 fictional children: 20 minutes, 1 adult,
2 devices, counters and trays. The minutes add up to the block, and every
child has exactly one task.

**Objective:** "Make a set of one to five objects that matches a request,
and check it by counting each object once."

| Minutes | Step | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Chef Capy model | Whole class. Read the opening script and count five objects aloud, touching each once. |
| 2–5 | Tray practice | Whole class. Each child builds a set of four on a tray; the adult watches how they count. |
| 5–17 | Centers | Four groups; details below. |
| 17–20 | Exit check | Individual: make five from a mixed pile and count it once. |

**Centers:**
- **Count carefully** (11 children, adult-led in two 3-minute turns): the
  adult models touching each piece once and saying the last number.
  Children make sets of 3, 4 and 5 and check them aloud.
- **Meet the task** (2 children, adult-led): the adult asks for 3, then 5,
  from a mixed pile and notes how they count.
- **Keep practicing** (3 children): counting orders on the 2 devices, in
  turns.
- **Try a new arrangement** (4 children): make five in two arrangements,
  then show two ways to split five.

**Opening script:** "Chef Capy needs five pieces for a customer. Watch me: I
touch each piece once — one, two, three, four, five. The last number I say
tells us how many. Now you make five for Chef Capy."

**Exit check (the physical transfer check):** "Ask each child, one at a
time: 'Make five for Chef Capy.' Mixed pile, no counting aloud by the adult.
Record: independent, with support, or needs another observation."

- The template allows about 45 seconds a child. With one adult, 4 children
  fit in 3 minutes; the other 16 are carried to the next block.
- These results are teacher observations: they are recorded apart from the
  game and never change its counts.

**Offline version of the activity:** "Paper sushi orders: ask for one to
five pieces; the child puts that many paper pieces on a paper plate and
counts them, touching each piece once."
