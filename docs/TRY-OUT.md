# Hands-on try-out — counting with Chef Capy (0.9.0)

A short, supervised try-out of the whole loop with the **fictional
profiles**, from a child's first order to the teacher's evidence and a
family report. About 20 minutes. It needs one device; a tablet held sideways
is the main case, and a phone either way up is worth a second pass.

Nothing here is filled in yet. Write only what you actually see. If a child
plays, use the fictional avatars (Mika, Ollie, Juno, Ren, Tavi, Sol) and do
not write the child's real name anywhere, here or in the app.

## 1. Enter and understand the first order

Tap an avatar outside the sushi bar. Chef Capy shows the task once: two
slices cut off the roll, one tapped onto the plate, the other sent with the
plate button, then the bell. (The skip button is at the top left.)

Watch for:

- Does the player look at the customer's card (the cat with a speech bubble)
  before doing anything?
- Is it clear that the bubble's maki are the cat's order, with no number to
  read?
- Does the demonstration show what the plate button and the bell are for?

Notes:

## 2. Prepare, adjust and submit a quantity

Cut the roll (swipe down across it, or press the knife), put pieces on the
plate (tap a slice, or the button with the piece going up to the plate),
take one back (tap it on the plate, or the button with the piece going down
to the board), then ring the bell.

Watch for:

- Does the player understand that only cut slices go on the plate, and that
  the rest of the roll stays on the board until it is sliced?
- Can they follow a piece as it hops between the board and the plate?
- Do they change the plate before ringing, as exploration, without worry?
- Is the bell clearly the thing to press when the plate is ready?
- Anything served by accident? (Nothing should be served until the bell.)

Notes:

## 3. Try a different number and recover with help

On a later order, deliberately put one piece too many (or too few) on the
plate and ring the bell.

Watch for:

- Does Chef Capy's counting together read as help, not as being wrong?
- Can the player adjust the plate afterwards and ring again?
- Does the cat look just as pleased when the second try matches?
- Try the Chef Capy button before answering on another order: does it feel
  like asking for help?

Notes:

## 4. Finish a round

Five orders make a round (one to five pieces, each once). At the end, choose
"more orders" (arrow) or finish (door).

Watch for:

- Is the end of the round clear without words?
- Do the two choices make sense?

Notes:

## 5. Find the evidence in the teacher workspace

Tap the grown-ups' button (top corner), answer the times-table question,
then **Teacher workspace**. Stay on **Prototype class (this device)**.

Watch for:

- Today: is the lesson action, then coverage, then what to do next, easy to
  scan? Are the denominators and dates where you expect them?
- Students → the avatar that played: does the profile's first sentence say
  what happened? The deliberate wrong answer should count as one independent
  answer that did not match, and the second try as a supported retry.
- Are the rule's details and every order still reachable behind
  "How the rule is met" and "Every counting order"?

Notes:

## 6. Prepare a lesson and preview a family report

On Today, **Prepare a lesson**. Open a section or the groups to edit them,
then **Teach this lesson** and step through it. Then **Reports**, choose the
same child and **Family summary**.

Watch for:

- Is the lesson readable as a plan before editing anything?
- Does the teaching view show what to do at each step, and is leaving it
  obvious?
- Does the family summary read as warm and modest, with nothing private?
- Is it clear that "Print or save this report as PDF" is this child's report
  and "Download class evidence (CSV)" is the whole class?
